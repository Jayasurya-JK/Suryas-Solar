**Short answer:** **yes, solar panels work in the rainy season**, just at lower output: roughly **30-60% of a sunny day** depending on cloud cover. And **yes, a well-built system survives cyclones**. In coastal Cuddalore and [Puducherry](/service-areas/puducherry) the weak point is almost never the panel; it is the **structure, the anchoring and the earthing**. Here is what every homeowner should know before the northeast monsoon.

---

## How Much Do Panels Produce in the Monsoon?

Tamil Nadu gets nearly half its yearly rain in the **northeast monsoon (October to December)**, so this is the lowest-output season.

| Weather | Output vs a clear day | 3kW system, units per day |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Clear sunny day | 100% | 13 - 15 |
| Light clouds | 70 - 85% | 10 - 12 |
| Heavy overcast | 30 - 60% | 5 - 8 |
| Continuous heavy rain | 10 - 25% | 2 - 4 |

Over a whole month even the monsoon averages **95-115 units per kW**, because there are always some sunny hours between rains. Summer then produces 150+ units per kW per month. With a **net meter**, your extra summer units are credited, so the yearly balance works out. ## Cyclones: What Actually Fails (and How to Prevent It)

Cuddalore and Puducherry have seen severe cyclones, from **Thane (2011)** to **Fengal (2024)**. When rooftop systems fail in storms, the cause is usually one of these:

| What fails | Why | How we prevent it |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Structure lifts or bends | Thin or poorly anchored frames; wind gets under the panels | **Hot-dip galvanized** steel structure designed for coastal wind loads as per **IS 875 (Part 3)**, anchored into the roof slab |
| Panels fly off | Loose or under-sized clamps | Correct clamps on every panel, **bolts torqued and marked**, checked before the monsoon |
| Rust weakens joints | Salt air on cheap pre-galvanized or painted steel | **Hot-dip galvanizing** (thicker zinc coat), stainless fasteners |
| Water damage to inverter | Inverter or DC box placed where rain or floodwater reaches | Inverter indoors or under cover, above flood level, IP65-rated boxes |

The **panel glass itself is tested for heavy loads** (international panel standards include static load tests far above normal wind pressure). A panel rarely breaks unless the structure under it moves.

## Lightning and Thunderstorms

Monsoon thunderstorms are a bigger daily risk than cyclones. A good system has:

*   **Three separate earthing pits**: one for the AC side, one for the DC side and one for the **lightning arrester (LA)**. Mixing them can send surge current back into your house wiring.
*   A **lightning arrester** mounted above the highest panel.
*   **Surge protection devices (SPDs)** in the DC and AC distribution boxes.

Why we insist on this: [solar earthing guide: why you need 3 separate pits](/blog/2026-01-13-solar-earthing-safety-guide).

## Pre-Monsoon Checklist for Solar Owners

Do these (or ask your installer to) before October:

1.  **Bolts and clamps:** every panel clamp tight, no rust at joints.
2.  **Structure base:** anchors firm, no cracks in the roof around the legs.
3.  **Cables:** no cracked insulation, cables clipped (not flapping), conduits sealed. Poor DC cables are a fire risk: [DC cable quality guide](/blog/2026-01-14-solar-dc-cable-quality-guide).
4.  **Earthing pits:** water the pits and check connections are not corroded.
5.  **Inverter location:** protected from rain and above any flood level.
6.  **Drainage:** terrace drains clear so water does not pool under the structure.
7.  **Clean once:** wash off salt and dust with plain water (no detergent, no high-pressure jet).

## During and After a Cyclone

*   **Before landfall:** if your area is under a cyclone warning or flooding is expected near the inverter, switch off the **AC and DC isolators** as your installer has shown you.
*   **During the storm:** stay indoors. **Never go to the roof.**
*   **After the storm:** look from a safe place. If panels are cracked, cables are hanging or the structure has moved, **do not touch anything**; call your installer.
*   **Insurance:** many home insurance policies can cover rooftop solar against storm damage. Keep your invoice and installation photos.

## Does Rain Cause Short Circuits? (IP Ratings Explained)

A properly installed system is built to sit in the rain for 25 years. What keeps water out:

| Part | Rating to look for | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Panel junction box | IP67 / IP68 | Dust-tight, survives temporary immersion |
| DC connectors (MC4 type) | IP67 or better, same brand both halves | Mismatched connectors leak and corrode |
| DC and AC distribution boxes | IP65 | Protected against rain jets |
| Inverter | IP65 (outdoor) or indoor placement | Rain-safe |

Problems come from **mismatched connectors, open cable entries and boxes mounted facing the rain**, not from rain itself. This is workmanship, which is why the installer matters more than the brand.

## 6 Questions to Ask Before You Buy Solar This Monsoon

1.  What **structure** do you use: hot-dip galvanized or pre-galvanized? What is the zinc thickness?
2.  How is the structure **anchored** to my roof, and will my roof waterproofing stay intact?
3.  How many **earthing pits**, and is there a **lightning arrester**?
4.  Where will the **inverter** be placed, and is it protected from rain and flooding?
5.  Are the **DC connectors** the same brand on both sides, and what is their IP rating?
6.  Do you offer a **pre-monsoon check** after installation?

If an installer gets vague on any of these, look elsewhere. See [7 solar scam warnings](/blog/2026-01-09-solar-scam-warnings-cuddalore).
