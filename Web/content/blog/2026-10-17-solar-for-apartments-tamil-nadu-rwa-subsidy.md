**Short answer:** yes, apartments in Tamil Nadu can go solar with a subsidy. Under PM Surya Ghar, a **Resident Welfare Association (RWA) or Group Housing Society (GHS)** gets **₹18,000 per kW** for a rooftop system that runs **common facilities** (lifts, water pumps, corridor and parking lights, EV charging). The subsidy covers up to **500 kW**, calculated at **3 kW per house**. Individual flats usually cannot install their own system unless they have an exclusive roof and their own connection.

> *Rules as reported in the PM Surya Ghar guidelines and consumer manuals, as of October 2026. We confirm the latest rules on the official portal before your association applies.*

## Apartment Solar Subsidy at a Glance

| Point | RWA / GHS (common facilities) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Subsidy rate | **₹18,000 per kW** |
| Maximum capacity | 500 kW |
| Capacity rule | Lower of (houses x 3 kW) or plant size |
| Includes | Lifts, pumps, common lights, EV charging, security, gym |
| Applicant | The registered RWA / society |
| Connection | Common-area EB connection only |
| Panels | DCR, installed by a registered vendor |

**Important:** if some residents already installed their own rooftop systems under the scheme, that capacity is counted inside the same cap.

## How Much Subsidy Can Your Apartment Get? (Examples)

| Apartment | Plant size | Eligible capacity | Subsidy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 12 flats | 10 kW | 10 kW (12 x 3 = 36 kW limit) | ₹1,80,000 |
| 24 flats | 20 kW | 20 kW | ₹3,60,000 |
| 8 flats | 30 kW | 24 kW (8 x 3 kW) | ₹4,32,000 |
| 60 flats | 50 kW | 50 kW | ₹9,00,000 |

The formula is simple: **subsidy = ₹18,000 x lower of (flats x 3 kW, plant kW)**, with a 500 kW ceiling.

## What Should the Apartment System Power?

Common-area loads are ideal for solar because most of them run in the daytime:

| Common load | Why solar fits |
| :--- | :--- |
| Water pumps (sump to overhead tank) | Can be scheduled for daytime solar hours |
| Lifts | Run all day; a big share of the common bill |
| Corridor, staircase and parking lights | Covered through net metering credit |
| Security cabin, gate motors, CCTV | Small, steady daytime load |
| EV chargers in parking | Included in the scheme's common facilities |

A system of about **1 kW for every 4 units of daily common-area use** is a good starting estimate. We size it from the last 12 months of common EB bills.

## Step by Step: How an RWA Goes Solar

1.  **Collect 12 months of common EB bills** and the terrace layout.
2.  **Free site survey:** we check shade-free terrace area, water tanks, structure and wind exposure. Roughly **80-100 sq ft per kW** is needed; see [how much roof space solar needs](/blog/2026-10-17-how-much-roof-space-for-solar-panels-sq-ft).
3.  **Association meeting:** approve the plan and budget, and authorise the secretary or treasurer to apply. Keep the minutes.
4.  **Register on the PM Surya Ghar portal** as an RWA/GHS with the common-area consumer number.
5.  **TNEB feasibility approval**, then installation by the registered vendor (usually 3-7 days for 10-30 kW).
6.  **Inspection and net meter** on the common connection.
7.  **Subsidy credit** to the association's bank account after commissioning.

The whole process typically takes **6-10 weeks**, the same as homes. See [solar installation timeline](/blog/2026-10-17-solar-installation-timeline-booking-to-net-meter).

## Can I Install Solar for My Own Flat?

In most apartments, **no**, because:
*   The terrace is **common property**, so every owner has a share in it.
*   Each flat's meter is in the meter room, often far from the terrace, so wiring separate systems is messy.
*   Shade from water tanks and lift rooms limits space.

It can work when your flat has an **exclusive terrace** (top-floor duplex, row house), your **own domestic connection**, and **written NOC from the association**. Then you apply as an individual home and can get the normal home subsidy; check yours with the [subsidy eligibility checker](/subsidy-eligibility).

## Savings Example: 24-Flat Apartment

*   Common-area use: about **80 units a day** (lifts, pumps, lights).
*   A **20 kW** system makes about **80-100 units a day** in Tamil Nadu.
*   Subsidy: **₹3,60,000** (20 kW x ₹18,000).
*   Net cost after subsidy is shared by 24 flats, and the lower common bill reduces monthly maintenance for everyone.

The exact saving depends on the common connection's tariff category; we calculate it from your actual bills.
