**Short answer:** a typical **2BHK home in Tamil Nadu with one AC** pays roughly **₹900-1,500 per two-month bill** in the cooler months and **₹2,500-4,250 in summer**. Homes **without an AC** that stay under **500 units** (per two months) often pay only **a few hundred rupees**, thanks to the **200 free units** introduced in May 2026. The real jump happens **above 500 units**, where bills can double. Here are typical numbers by home size.

> *Bills are bimonthly. Slab rates below are approximate (the same figures used in our [TNEB slab rates guide](/blog/2026-04-30-how-to-calculate-eb-bill-tamil-nadu-examples)); always confirm with your own TNPDCL bill.*

## Typical Units and Bills by Home Type (Two-Month Cycle)

| Home | Typical units (2 months) | Approx. bill (2 months) | Approx. per month |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1BHK, no AC | 150 - 250 | ₹100 - ₹350 | ₹50 - ₹175 |
| 2BHK, no AC | 250 - 400 | ₹325 - ₹1,000 | ₹160 - ₹500 |
| 2BHK, 1 AC (cool months) | 380 - 480 | ₹900 - ₹1,500 | ₹450 - ₹750 |
| 2BHK, 1 AC (summer) | 550 - 750 | ₹2,450 - ₹4,250 | ₹1,225 - ₹2,125 |
| 3BHK, 2 ACs (summer) | 800 - 1,200 | ₹4,650 - ₹8,850 | ₹2,325 - ₹4,425 |
| Large home / joint family, 3+ ACs | 1,200 - 1,800 | ₹8,850 - ₹15,450 | ₹4,425 - ₹7,725 |

Bills include an approximate fixed charge of about ₹100-150. Your actual bill depends on appliances, family size, and how many hours the AC runs.

## How the Slabs Create These Numbers

**If you use 500 units or less (per two months):**
*   0 - 200 units: **free**
*   201 - 400 units: about **₹4.50** per unit
*   401 - 500 units: about **₹6.00** per unit

**If you cross 500 units:**
*   Only 0 - 100 units free
*   101 - 400: about ₹4.50 · 401 - 500: about ₹6.00 · 501 - 600: about ₹8.00 · 601 - 800: about ₹9.00 · 801 - 1,000: about ₹10.00 · above 1,000: about ₹11.00

### Worked examples
*   **350 units:** 150 × ₹4.50 = ₹675 + fixed charge ≈ **₹775**
*   **480 units:** 200 × ₹4.50 + 80 × ₹6.00 = ₹1,380 + fixed ≈ **₹1,480**
*   **750 units:** 300 × ₹4.50 + 100 × ₹6 + 100 × ₹8 + 150 × ₹9 = ₹4,100 + fixed ≈ **₹4,250**
*   **1,000 units:** ₹1,350 + ₹600 + ₹800 + ₹1,800 + ₹2,000 = ₹6,550 + fixed ≈ **₹6,700**

Notice the cliff: going from **480 to 750 units** (about 56% more electricity) almost **triples** the bill. That is why summer AC use hurts so much. Read [why the EB bill is high in summer](/blog/2026-04-27-why-is-electricity-bill-high-in-summer-tamil-nadu).

## Is My Bill "Normal"? A Quick Self-Check

1.  **Find your units** on the bill (current reading minus previous reading).
2.  **Compare with the table** above for your home type and season.
3.  If your units are **30%+ above** the typical range with no new appliance, check for:
    *   An old refrigerator or AC (low star rating)
    *   A water pump running too long, or a leaking tank float valve
    *   Earth leakage in old wiring (an electrician can test this)
    *   A wrong meter reading (compare the meter display with the bill)

More tips: [how to reduce your electricity bill in Tamil Nadu](/blog/2026-04-28-how-to-reduce-electricity-bill-tamil-nadu-solar).

## 5 Cheap Ways to Stay Under the 500-Unit Cliff

If your bimonthly units sit just above 500, small changes can save hundreds of rupees:

1.  **Set the AC to 24-26°C** instead of 18-20°C. Each degree warmer saves roughly 5-6% of AC power.
2.  **Use a timer or sleep mode** so the AC switches off in the early morning.
3.  **Clean AC filters every 2 weeks** in summer; dirty filters make the compressor run longer.
4.  **Run the water pump with a timer** and check the tank float valve for overflow.
5.  **Replace old tube lights and fans** with LED lights and BLDC fans; they use half the power or less.

For homes well above 500 units, these help but solar is what really changes the bill.
