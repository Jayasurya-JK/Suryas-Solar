## Do You Actually Need a Solar Battery?

This is the most important question, and many buyers get it wrong.

| Your situation | What you need | Battery? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Want lower EB bill, power cuts are rare | **On-grid system + net meter** | **No** |
| Frequent cuts, need fans, lights, Wi-Fi, fridge | **Hybrid system** | Yes |
| No EB connection at all (farm shed, remote site) | **Off-grid system** | Yes |
| Already have a home inverter + battery | On-grid solar; keep your existing UPS | No new battery |

**On-grid saves the most money.** A battery adds cost, needs replacement every 5-10 years, and is not covered by the subsidy. But where power cuts genuinely disrupt life or work, a hybrid system with a good battery is worth it. Read [on-grid vs hybrid solar](/blog/2026-01-06-on-grid-vs-hybrid-solar-tamil-nadu) and [voltage problems and hybrid solar](/blog/2026-01-15-voltage-fluctuation-hybrid-solar).

## Tubular vs Lithium Solar Batteries

| | Solar tubular (lead-acid) | Lithium (LFP) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher (about 2-3x) |
| Life | About 4-6 years with good care | About 8-12 years |
| Maintenance | Distilled water top-up every few months | None |
| Depth of discharge | Use about 50% safely | Use about 80-90% |
| Weight and space | Heavy, needs ventilated space | Compact, wall-mountable |
| Best for | Budget backup, light loads | Daily cycling, larger backup |

For a typical Cuddalore home wanting backup for fans, lights, TV and Wi-Fi during cuts, a **solar tubular battery** is the budget choice. If you want to run a fridge and an AC for some hours, or you cycle the battery every day, **lithium** is better value over its life.

## Battery Care Tips for Tamil Nadu Heat

*   Keep batteries in a **shaded, ventilated** place, never on a hot terrace. Heat shortens battery life.
*   For tubular batteries, **check water levels** every 2-3 months and use only distilled water.
*   Avoid draining a tubular battery below about 50% regularly.
*   Make sure the battery and inverter are **properly earthed**. See our [earthing guide](/blog/2026-01-13-solar-earthing-safety-guide).
