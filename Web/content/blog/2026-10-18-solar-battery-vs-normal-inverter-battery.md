**Short answer:** a **solar battery** is designed for **daily charging from solar and deep, daily discharge**, and is usually rated at **C10**. A **normal inverter battery** is designed for **occasional power cuts with grid charging**, usually rated at **C20**. A normal battery can work with solar, but it **wears out faster** when cycled every day. For a hybrid solar system, choose a **solar tubular** battery on a budget, or **lithium (LFP)** for the longest life and no maintenance. For a plain **on-grid system, you need no battery at all**.

## C10 vs C20: What the Label Means

Battery capacity (Ah) is quoted at a certain **discharge rate**:
*   **C20** = capacity measured over a slow 20-hour discharge (typical of normal inverter batteries).
*   **C10** = capacity measured over a faster 10-hour discharge (typical of solar batteries).

A 150Ah **C10** battery delivers its rated capacity at a faster discharge than a 150Ah **C20** battery, so the C10 battery suits solar loads better. Solar batteries also accept the **changing charge current** from panels through the day, which is harder on standard batteries.

## Can You Use Your Existing Inverter Battery With Solar?

| Your situation | Advice |
| :--- | :--- |
| You already have a home UPS and want on-grid solar | **Keep both separate.** On-grid solar needs no battery; your UPS covers cuts as before. |
| You want a small solar charger for your UPS battery | Possible with a solar charge controller, but expect shorter battery life with daily cycling |
| You want a proper hybrid system for backup | Use **solar tubular or lithium** batteries matched to the hybrid inverter |

Most Tamil Nadu homes get the best value from **on-grid solar plus their existing UPS**, and add a hybrid later only if cuts are frequent. Compare: [on-grid vs hybrid solar](/blog/2026-01-06-on-grid-vs-hybrid-solar-tamil-nadu).

## How to Choose the Right Battery

1.  **Decide if you need backup at all.** Short, rare cuts: skip the battery and go on-grid.
2.  **List your essential loads** (fans, lights, fridge, Wi-Fi) and the hours of backup you want.
3.  **Size in kWh:** for 600 W of essential load and 6 hours, about 3.6 kWh usable.
4.  **Match the inverter:** the battery must be on the hybrid inverter's approved list.
5.  **Check warranty in years and cycles**, and who services it locally.
6.  **Plan the location:** shaded, ventilated, above flood level.

## Battery Care in Tamil Nadu Heat

*   Keep batteries **indoors and out of direct sun**; heat shortens battery life.
*   **Tubular batteries:** check distilled water every 2-3 months and keep terminals clean.
*   **Lithium batteries:** no water, but keep them within the temperature range on the datasheet.
*   During long monsoon cloudy spells, let the grid top up a hybrid battery so it does not sit empty.

## Common Mistakes With Solar Batteries

1.  **Buying a battery for an on-grid system.** On-grid inverters do not use batteries; the money is wasted.
2.  **Oversizing the battery.** Size for essential loads and real cut hours, not the whole house.
3.  **Mixing old and new batteries** in one bank, which shortens the life of the new ones.
4.  **Keeping batteries in a hot, closed room**, which cuts their life sharply.
