**Short answer:** do not compare solar quotes by the final price alone. Put them side by side and check **12 items**: panel brand, model, wattage and **DCR status**; **inverter** brand and warranty; **structure** material and height; **DC cable** brand; number of **earthing pits** and the **lightning arrester**; **warranties**; **subsidy and TNEB paperwork**; **net meter fees**; **timeline**; and **payment terms**. Then compare **price per watt**. A quote that is ₹20,000 cheaper usually has a missing or downgraded part.

## The 12 Checks Explained

### 1. System size matches your bill
A quote should explain **why** 3kW or 5kW, from your EB bills. Oversized systems waste money; undersized ones leave a bill. Check with the [solar savings calculator](/calc).

### 2. Panel brand, model and wattage
"Tier-1 panels" is not enough. Get the **exact model** and wattage. Compare: [solar panel price list by brand](/blog/2026-10-17-solar-panel-price-list-tamil-nadu-by-brand).

### 3. DCR status in writing
For the **₹1 lakh subsidy**, panels must be **DCR**. Ask for the DCR certificate. Non-DCR panels lose the whole subsidy.

### 4. Inverter brand and warranty
BIS-registered, a known brand with **local service**, 5-10 year warranty. See [best on-grid solar inverter](/blog/2026-10-17-best-on-grid-solar-inverter-india).

### 5. Structure material and height
In [Cuddalore](/service-areas/cuddalore)'s coastal air, **hot-dip galvanized** steel lasts; plain GI or painted MS rusts. A raised structure keeps your terrace usable. See [best solar structure for coastal Tamil Nadu](/blog/2025-12-02-rooftop-solar-structure-types-tamil-nadu).

### 6. DC cable brand and size
Solar-grade copper DC cable from a known brand. Cheap cable heats and is a fire risk. See [solar wire quality](/blog/2026-01-14-solar-dc-cable-quality-guide).

### 7. Earthing and lightning arrester
**Three separate pits** (DC, AC, lightning arrester) plus a lightning arrester. One shared pit is a common cost cut. See [solar earthing guide](/blog/2026-01-13-solar-earthing-safety-guide).

### 8. Warranties in writing
Panel product and performance, inverter, and **installer workmanship**. Read [solar panel warranty explained](/blog/2026-10-17-solar-panel-warranty-explained-25-years).

### 9. Subsidy and paperwork
Is the company a **registered PM Surya Ghar vendor**? Who handles registration, TNEB feasibility, inspection and subsidy follow-up? Check your eligibility first with the [subsidy eligibility checker](/subsidy-eligibility).

### 10. Fees included or extra
Net meter charges, TNEB fees, load increase, transport, and extra cable for a long run to the meter. "Extra at actuals" can add ₹5,000-15,000.

### 11. Timeline
A clear plan: installation within days of feasibility approval and support until the net meter is fixed. See [solar installation timeline](/blog/2026-10-17-solar-installation-timeline-booking-to-net-meter).

### 12. Payment terms
A reasonable advance, a main payment on material delivery, and the balance after installation. **Never 100% upfront.**

## Worked Example: Why ₹20,000 Cheaper Is Not Cheaper

| | Quote A | Quote B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Price (3kW) | ₹1,95,000 | ₹1,75,000 |
| Panels | DCR TOPCon 580 W | Non-DCR 540 W |
| Structure | Hot-dip galvanized, raised | Plain GI, low |
| Earthing | 3 pits + LA | 1 pit, no LA |
| Subsidy | ₹1,00,000 (eligible home) | **₹0** (non-DCR) |
| **Real cost to you** | **₹95,000** | **₹1,75,000** |

The "cheaper" quote costs **₹80,000 more**, and its structure may rust within a few years near the coast.

## Price Per Watt: The Fair Comparison

Divide the total price by the system watts. For example, ₹1,95,000 ÷ 3,000 W = **₹65 per watt** installed. In 2026, complete on-grid DCR systems in Tamil Nadu are typically around **₹60-70 per watt** for 3kW, and a bit lower per watt for larger systems. A price far below this range needs an explanation.

## Questions to Ask Every Installer

1.  Are you registered on the PM Surya Ghar portal? Under what name?
2.  How many systems have you installed in my district?
3.  Can I see one of your installations or speak to a customer?
4.  Who will do the installation: your own team or subcontractors?
5.  What happens if the inverter fails in year 3? Who comes, and how fast?
6.  Is everything in the quote, or will there be extras?
