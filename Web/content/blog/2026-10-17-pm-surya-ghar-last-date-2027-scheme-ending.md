**Short answer:** the **PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana is approved till 31 March 2027** (the end of financial year 2026-27). As of **October 2026, no extension has been officially notified**. In Tamil Nadu there is a second clock: the **₹22,000 state top-up** is reported to be limited to the **first 1 lakh eligible homes**, so it may run out even earlier.

If you want the full **₹1 lakh subsidy** (₹78,000 + ₹22,000), the safe plan is to **register and install by December 2026 or early January 2027**.

> *Status as of 17 October 2026. We will update this page if MNRE or the Tamil Nadu government announces any change.*

## Is the Scheme Really Ending? What We Know

*   **Approved period:** The scheme was approved to run up to FY 2026-27. Its total outlay is ₹75,021 crore.
*   **Progress:** News reports in October 2026 said about **60 lakh homes** had benefited, against the 1 crore target. Official projections reported in September 2026 expected **85-90 lakh homes by March 2027**, short of the target.
*   **Extension talk:** Some reports say the Centre is considering a **3-4 month extension** and a successor scheme. **Neither has been notified.** Subsidy amounts after March 2027 are not guaranteed.
*   **Not the same deadline:** You may have seen "30 November 2026" in the news. That deadline is for old Phase-II rooftop programme claims, not for new PM Surya Ghar applications.

**Our advice:** treat 31 March 2027 as the real deadline. If an extension comes, it is a bonus, not a plan.

## Why Waiting Until February Is Risky

Here is the typical timeline for a home in Cuddalore district:

| Step | Typical time |
| :--- | :--- |
| Site visit, design, quote | 1 - 3 days |
| Portal registration + TNEB feasibility approval | 1 - 2 weeks |
| Installation | 1 - 3 days |
| Inspection + net meter installation | 2 - 6 weeks |
| Subsidy credit after commissioning | about 30 days |
| **Total** | **6 - 10 weeks** |

Near every deadline, three queues grow at the same time: installers, TNEB inspections and net meter supply. A family that starts in late February can easily miss 31 March.

There is also a cost reason. A reported exemption that lets PM Surya Ghar systems use panels without ALMM List-II (approved Indian cells) ends on **31 December 2026**. After that, eligible panels may cost more. Installing earlier can lock in today's prices.

## Three Scenarios After March 2027

Nobody outside the government knows the final decision. Here is how each possibility affects you:

| Scenario | What it means for you |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Scheme ends on 31 March 2027** | Only systems commissioned (and claims filed) in time get ₹78,000. Late starters get nothing from the central scheme. |
| **Short extension (reported 3-4 months)** | More time, but subsidy rates and rules could change, and the rush continues. |
| **Successor scheme** | New rules, possibly a different subsidy amount. Past schemes have often reduced per-kW support over time. |

In every scenario, **a home that is commissioned before March 2027 is safe**. That is the only option without risk.

## 6 Mistakes That Delay Subsidy Near Deadlines

1.  **Name mismatch** between Aadhaar, EB bill and bank account. Fix this first; it is the most common delay.
2.  **Bank account not Aadhaar-seeded** for direct transfers. Ask your bank to link it.
3.  **Choosing an unregistered installer.** Only vendors registered on the PM Surya Ghar portal can be selected, and only their installations get the subsidy.
4.  **Non-DCR panels.** The panels must be DCR (made with Indian cells). Check the model on the invoice. See [Premier vs Adani](/blog/2026-10-17-premier-vs-adani-solar-panel-which-is-better) for how to verify.
5.  **Sanctioned load too low.** If your system size is larger than your sanctioned load, TNEB may ask you to increase it first, which adds weeks.
6.  **Waiting for the net meter without following up.** Inspection and meter fixing are the slowest steps; your installer should track them for you.
