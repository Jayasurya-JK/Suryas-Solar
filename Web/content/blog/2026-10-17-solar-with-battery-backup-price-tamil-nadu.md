**Short answer:** in Tamil Nadu, a **3kW solar system with battery backup costs about ₹2.6 to 3.2 lakh** with a tubular battery bank, or **₹3.2 to 4 lakh with a 5kWh lithium battery** (2026 market range). The **solar part still gets the PM Surya Ghar subsidy** if the system is grid-connected, but the **battery is never subsidised**. If your area has only occasional short power cuts, a plain on-grid system (₹1.8-2.1 lakh) gives a much faster payback.

> *Prices are market averages as of October 2026, not our quotes. Battery prices vary a lot by brand, chemistry and capacity; we give a written quote after a free site visit.*

The difference between on-grid and hybrid comes from three parts: the **hybrid inverter** (costlier than an on-grid one), the **battery bank**, and extra wiring for a separate "backup" distribution board.

## Why Battery Prices Vary So Much

### 1. Battery chemistry
| | Tubular lead-acid | Lithium (LFP) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Upfront cost | Lower | Higher (roughly 2-3x per usable kWh) |
| Usable capacity | About 50% of rated | About 80-90% of rated |
| Life | 4 - 6 years typical | 8 - 10+ years typical |
| Maintenance | Distilled water top-up, ventilation | None |
| Space and weight | Large and heavy | Compact, wall or rack mounted |

Lithium looks expensive, but because you can use more of its capacity and it lasts about twice as long, the **10-year cost is often lower** for homes that use the battery every day. Tubular batteries make sense when cuts are rare and budget is tight.

### Battery price by size (2026 market range)

| Battery | Stores (usable) | Approx. price | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 150Ah 12V solar tubular | about 0.9 kWh | ₹13,000 - ₹18,000 | Common for small backup; 2-4 needed for a hybrid bank |
| 200Ah 12V solar tubular | about 1.2 kWh | ₹17,000 - ₹23,000 | Longer backup per battery |
| 5kWh lithium (LFP) pack | about 4.5 kWh | ₹1.1 - 1.6 lakh | Wall or rack mounted, no maintenance |
| 10kWh lithium (LFP) pack | about 9 kWh | ₹2 - 3 lakh | Larger homes, longer cuts |

*Usable energy assumes about 50% depth of discharge for tubular and about 90% for lithium. Prices vary by brand, warranty and voltage; confirm in a written quote.*

### 2. Battery size (kWh)
Battery capacity is measured in **kWh (units)**. A 5kWh battery stores about 5 units. If your essential load is 600 watts, that is roughly 7-8 hours of backup on a lithium battery.

### 3. Inverter type
A hybrid inverter manages solar, battery and grid together. Good hybrids also handle **voltage fluctuation**, which is common in parts of Panruti and rural Cuddalore. See [voltage fluctuation and hybrid solar](/blog/2026-01-15-voltage-fluctuation-hybrid-solar).

## How Much Backup Do You Really Need?

Most families do not need the whole house on battery. Make a list of **essential loads**:

| Load | Typical watts |
| :--- | :--- |
| 4 ceiling fans (BLDC) | 120 - 140 W |
| 6 LED lights | 60 - 80 W |
| Refrigerator | 100 - 200 W (average) |
| TV + set-top box | 100 - 150 W |
| Wi-Fi router, phone chargers | 30 - 50 W |
| **Total essential load** | **about 450 - 650 W** |

At about 600 W, a **5kWh lithium battery** gives roughly **7 hours**, which covers almost any TNEB cut. An **AC (1.2-1.5 kW)** would empty the same battery in about 3 hours, so we usually keep ACs off the backup board. Read [can a 1.5 ton AC run on solar](/blog/2026-04-25-can-1-5-ton-ac-run-on-solar-power-tamil-nadu).

## Subsidy With a Battery: What Exactly Applies

*   The **PM Surya Ghar central subsidy** (up to ₹78,000) and the **Tamil Nadu top-up** (up to ₹22,000 for eligible homes) are for the **grid-connected solar capacity**. Grid-connected hybrid systems are reported as eligible; pure off-grid systems are not.
*   The **battery, its rack and backup wiring** are not subsidised.
*   Panels must still be **DCR** and installed by a **registered vendor**.

So a 3kW hybrid with lithium battery at about ₹3.6 lakh could come down to about **₹2.6 lakh** after the full ₹1 lakh subsidy. ## When Battery Backup Is Worth It (and When It Is Not)

**Worth it if:**
*   You face **frequent or long power cuts** (rural areas, monsoon season, cyclone-hit areas).
*   Someone at home **works from home** or a family member needs **medical equipment**.
*   Your voltage is unstable and appliances keep tripping.
*   You already spend on a regular inverter-UPS and its batteries every few years.

**Not worth it if:**
*   Cuts in your area are short and rare (many town areas in Cuddalore).
*   Your main goal is the **lowest EB bill and fastest payback**. Net metering already works like a free "battery": TNEB stores your extra daytime units.

Many families take a middle path: **on-grid solar now** for the subsidy and savings, plus their **existing home UPS** for cuts. A hybrid can be added later. Compare both in [on-grid vs hybrid solar](/blog/2026-01-06-on-grid-vs-hybrid-solar-tamil-nadu).

## Battery Safety and Care in Coastal Tamil Nadu

1.  **Keep batteries indoors**, in a shaded, ventilated spot, away from kitchen heat. Heat shortens battery life.
2.  **Lithium batteries need a proper BMS** (battery management system) and must match the inverter's approved battery list.
3.  **Tubular batteries need water top-up** every 2-3 months and a ventilated corner for gases.
4.  **Above flood level:** in low-lying parts of Cuddalore, mount the battery and inverter above any monsoon water level. See [solar panels in rain and cyclones](/blog/2026-10-17-solar-panels-rain-cyclone-cuddalore-monsoon-guide).
5.  **Check warranty in years and cycles**, and who services it locally.
