**Short answer:** in Tamil Nadu, plan for about **80-100 sq ft of shade-free roof per kW**. A **3kW system needs about 250-300 sq ft**, a **5kW system about 400-500 sq ft**, and a **10kW system about 700-1,000 sq ft**, using modern 540-620 W panels. The exact space depends on panel wattage, the roof shape, shade from water tanks and coconut trees, and whether you use a raised structure.

## Panel Size: How Big Is One Panel?

| Panel type | Approx. size | Area |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 440 W (older Mono PERC) | about 1.9 m x 1.13 m | about 23 sq ft |
| 540 - 580 W | about 2.27 m x 1.13 m | about 27 - 28 sq ft |
| 600 - 620 W (TOPCon) | about 2.38 m x 1.13 m | about 29 sq ft |

Higher-wattage panels produce **more power from the same roof**, so on a small terrace they let you fit more kW. Compare panel types: [bifacial vs monocrystalline](/blog/2026-01-07-mono-vs-bifacial-solar-panels).

## Why "Shade-Free" Matters More Than Area

Even a small shadow can cut output, because panels in a string are connected together. Common shade sources on Tamil Nadu terraces:
*   **Overhead water tanks** (Sintex tanks)
*   **Staircase head rooms** and lift rooms
*   **Coconut and neem trees**
*   **Neighbouring taller buildings**
*   **Parapet walls** and dish antennas

In winter (December-January) the sun is lower in the south, so shadows are **longer** than in summer. A proper check looks at shade across the year: [solar shadow analysis guide](/blog/2026-01-12-solar-shadow-analysis-guide-cuddalore).

## Which Direction Should Panels Face?

In Tamil Nadu, panels should face **south** with a tilt of about **10-15 degrees**. Flat terraces make this easy. East-west layouts also work with a small loss, which helps on narrow or oddly shaped roofs.

## Raised Structure vs Low Structure

| | Low structure (1-2 ft) | Raised structure (6-8 ft) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Terrace use | Mostly blocked | Usable underneath |
| Cooling effect on top floor | Some | Good shade on roof slab |
| Shade from parapet / tanks | More likely | Panels sit above many shadows |
| Cost | Lower | Higher (more steel) |
| Wind load | Lower | Needs stronger design for cyclones |

Most families in Cuddalore prefer a **raised, hot-dip galvanized structure** so they can still use the terrace. See [best solar structure for coastal Tamil Nadu](/blog/2025-12-02-rooftop-solar-structure-types-tamil-nadu).

## Small Terrace? 6 Ways to Fit More Solar

1.  **Use 580-620 W panels** instead of 440 W.
2.  **Build over the staircase room or water tank** with a raised frame.
3.  **Move the water tank** to one side or a lower platform if possible.
4.  **Portrait vs landscape layout:** try both on the drawing; one often fits an extra panel.
5.  **East-west split** across a narrow terrace.
6.  **Bifacial panels on a raised frame** over a white-painted roof get a few extra units from reflected light.

If space allows only 2kW, it can still give a big saving and a ₹70,000 subsidy. See [2kW solar system](/blog/2026-10-17-2kw-solar-system-units-per-day-price-subsidy).

## Sloped, Sheet and Tile Roofs

*   **Concrete flat terrace:** easiest, most common in Tamil Nadu.
*   **Metal sheet roof:** panels can be fixed with rail and clamp systems if the purlins are strong.
*   **Mangalore tile roof:** usually needs a separate frame or an elevated structure; tiles break easily.
*   **Asbestos sheet:** not recommended to drill; use a separate frame.

## Roof Load: Is My Roof Strong Enough?

Panels and a standard structure add about **15-20 kg per square metre**, which a normal RCC roof handles easily. Older roofs with cracks or leaks should be repaired and waterproofed **before** installation, because removing panels later costs money.

## Quick Self-Check Before Calling an Installer

1.  Measure your terrace length and breadth (in feet).
2.  Subtract the area of the water tank, staircase room and any space that gets shade after 9 AM or before 4 PM.
3.  Divide the remaining area by 90 to get a rough maximum kW.
4.  Compare with your EB bill using the [solar savings calculator](/calc), and check your subsidy with the [eligibility checker](/subsidy-eligibility).
